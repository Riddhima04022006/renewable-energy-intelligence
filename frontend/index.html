India | Electricity Sector

Understanding India's Electricity Transition

An analytical exploration of how electricity generation, renewable energy contributions, installed capacity and power-sector emissions vary across India and evolve over time.

National dataset coverage 2019–2025 Monthly observations from January 2019 to November 2025.
Regional analysis period 2021–2025 Common state and UT comparison period: July 2021–November 2025.
Reporting regions 36 28 States and 8 Union Territories, using Ember reporting geography.
Original dataset records 238,200 Monthly generation, capacity and power-sector emissions records.

Research Question

How has the electricity generation mix across Indian States and Union Territories evolved over time, and how have renewable energy sources contributed to changes in regional and national electricity generation patterns?

Analytical Scope

The project examines national generation trends, state-level renewable penetration, individual renewable sources, installed capacity, capacity factors and power-sector emissions.

How to Interpret This Analysis

Absolute generation indicates the scale of electricity produced. Generation shares describe the relative contribution of each source group to total recorded generation. These are different measures: an increase in renewable generation does not automatically imply an equal increase in renewable share.

Analytical Considerations

Generation and composition answer different questions. Generation measures how much electricity a source produces, while composition measures its contribution to total renewable generation. A source's composition share can decline even when its absolute generation increases, if other sources grow faster.

Analysis A4

Installed Capacity and Power-Sector Emissions

Examine renewable installed capacity, source-level capacity factors, absolute power-sector emissions and reported CO2 intensity.

Capacity Analysis

Installed capacity represents the maximum rated generation capability of the recorded assets. Capacity factor compares actual generation with the theoretical generation possible at installed capacity over the observation period.

Installed Capacity Trend

National renewable installed capacity over time.

Capacity by Renewable Source

Installed capacity composition.

Capacity Factor by Source

Generation relative to installed capacity.

Capacity factor is calculated as: Capacity Factor (%) = Generation (GWh) × 1000 / [Capacity (MW) × Hours in Month] × 100 Values above 100% are treated as flagged observations, not automatically as errors. Known anomalous series require separate consideration in regional comparisons.

Power-Sector Emissions

Absolute emissions and emissions intensity provide different perspectives. Absolute emissions measure the reported emissions quantity, while CO2 intensity relates emissions to electricity generation. Neither measure alone establishes that changes in renewable generation caused changes in emissions.

The analytical results are based on a structured review of the source dataset, coverage, missing observations, consistency and derived metrics.

Data Quality Audit

D1 | Dataset Structure

Examined dimensions, fields, geographic labels, categories, variables, units and date coverage.

D2 | Temporal Coverage

Checked monthly coverage and gaps across geographic reporting units and measurement series.

D3 | Missing and Invalid Values

Identified blank values, non-numeric observations and metric-specific availability limitations.

D4 | Zero-Value Analysis

Examined zero observations and their distribution. Zero values were not automatically treated as missing.

D5 | Aggregate Consistency

Tested arithmetic relationships between aggregate variables and their reported components.

D6 | Percentage Validation

Compared reported generation percentages with percentages recalculated from absolute generation.

D7 | Derived Metric Validation

Examined calculated capacity factors, fuel emissions intensity and reported CO2 intensity.

D8 | Geographic Reconciliation

Compared the sum of state and UT observations with India Total, accounting for Others and incomplete coverage.

D9 | Outlier Screening

Applied an IQR-based screening procedure to identify unusual observations for review.

D10 | Unresolved Data Questions

Documented unresolved source definitions, reporting changes, unit inconsistencies and other limitations.

Analytical Definitions

Renewable Share

Renewables Generation / Total Generation × 100

Calculated from absolute GWh values rather than relying on the reported percentage field.

Fossil Share

Fossil Generation / Total Generation × 100

Represents the fossil contribution to total recorded electricity generation.

State Contribution

State Renewable Generation / India Renewable Generation × 100

Measures each state's contribution to national renewable generation.

Year-on-Year Change

(Current Month − Previous-Year Month) / Previous-Year Month × 100

Compares the same calendar month across years. Undefined when the baseline is zero or unavailable.

Important Interpretation Limits

Generation is not Consumption

The analysis concerns recorded electricity generation. It does not represent the complete electricity consumed within a state, including electricity imported from other regions.

Reporting Geography

Historical reporting units may differ from current administrative boundaries. Combined historical values must not be interpreted as independent observations for each region.

Incomplete Observations

Missing records are not automatically treated as zero. Metric-specific availability is considered when calculating comparisons.

Partial Year 2025

National data ends in November 2025. Annual comparisons involving 2025 must account for its incomplete coverage.

Capacity Factor Flags

Observations above 100% are flagged for investigation. They are not automatically assumed to be measurement errors.

Emissions Interpretation

Emissions trends are descriptive. The analysis does not establish a causal relationship between renewable generation and emissions.

Others Reporting Category

The Others category is not assigned to a geographic region or redistributed across States and Union Territories.

Source and Measurement Uncertainty

Internal arithmetic consistency does not independently establish the accuracy of underlying source measurements.
